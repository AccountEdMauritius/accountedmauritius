O Level · Chapter 1: Fundamentals of Accounting

Nadia starts a business. She has cash of Rs 40,000 and equipment worth Rs 25,000. She still owes her supplier Rs 15,000 for the equipment.

(a) Calculate Nadia's owner's equity.

(b) Nadia pays the supplier Rs 5,000 from cash. Show the accounting equation after this payment.

(a) Assets = Rs 40,000 + Rs 25,000 = Rs 65,000.
Liabilities = Rs 15,000.
Owner's equity = Assets − Liabilities = Rs 65,000 − Rs 15,000 = Rs 50,000.

(b) After the payment, cash falls to Rs 35,000 and the amount owed falls to Rs 10,000.

Assets
Rs 35,000 + Rs 25,000
Rs 60,000= Liabilities
Rs 15,000 − Rs 5,000
Rs 10,000+ Owner's equity
no change
Rs 50,000

Remember: paying a debt lowers assets and liabilities by the same amount, so owner's equity stays the same. Check: Rs 60,000 = Rs 10,000 + Rs 50,000.

A Level · Chapter 1: Types of Business Entity

Amina runs her shop as a sole trader. She is thinking of turning it into a private limited company.

(a) State two differences between a sole trader and a private limited company. (2 marks)

(b) Explain one risk that Amina carries now that she would no longer carry personally if she became a company. (2 marks)

(a) Any two of these:

  • Liability: a sole trader has unlimited liability. The shareholders of a company have limited liability.
  • Legal status: a sole trader and the business are the same person in law. A company is a separate legal entity, so it can own assets, owe money and be sued in its own name.
  • Capital: a sole trader depends on personal savings and loans. A company can raise capital by issuing shares to other investors.
  • Continuity: a company carries on if a shareholder dies or sells their shares. A sole trader's business ends with the owner.

(b) As a sole trader, Amina has unlimited liability. If the shop owes more than its assets are worth, creditors can claim her personal assets, such as her car or savings, to settle the debts. As a shareholder of a company, she could lose only the money she put into it.

Remember: in a two-mark "explain" question, give the point and then say what it means for Amina.
